§1 · Foundational Demarcation & Research Roadmap

Two Regimes of Network Propagation

Distinguishing physical mass-energy conservation from institutional leverage multiplication — and establishing what counts as an empirical contribution.

The mathematical analysis of systemic fragility requires drawing an uncompromising boundary between two regimes that are frequently conflated in informal applied graph literature: conservative flow networks and non-conservative loss-multiplication lattices.

In physical engineering systems — such as high-pressure natural gas transmission grids, petrochemical trunk pipelines, and regional water aqueducts — the governing equations are dictated by the fundamental laws of physics: mass, momentum, and energy are conserved quantities. Fluid cannot spontaneously vanish or multiply inside a junction; every barrel of oil or cubic meter of gas that enters a manifold must exit through connected branches or accumulate according to thermodynamic state equations. Consequently, physical networks belong to the domain of circulations, cycle spaces, and totally unimodular incidence matrices (van Lint & Wilson, 2001, Ch. 7). A pipe rupture or compressor tripping does not destroy the physical fluid in existence elsewhere; rather, it alters boundary constraints, redistributes pressures, and saturates alternative network cut sets according to the Max-Flow Min-Cut Theorem (Ford & Fulkerson, 1956).

In financial credit markets, however, contagion operates through a non-conservative, subadditive mechanism. Interbank assets represent bilateral claims that depend circularly on the solvency of counterparties. When an institution enters default, its nominal liabilities are cleared under limited liability and absolute priority, inducing a pro-rata haircut on its creditors. This process is mathematically formalized not by linear conservation of flow, but by fixed-point operators on complete lattices under Tarski's Theorem (Eisenberg & Noe, 2001). Furthermore, when financial institutions operate with high leverage, the write-down of an interbank asset wipes out the creditor's equity capital. Through DebtRank dynamics (Battiston et al., 2012), equity destroyed by a shock is eliminated from the economy — it is not conserved. A \$1 primary insolvency shock can destroy \$4.20 of aggregate banking equity through balance-sheet multiplier feedback loops (K11).

As formulated by Lofrano (2026), unifying these two domains requires acknowledging this mathematical dualism: while the underlying physical topology and institutional exposures are both represented as directed weighted graphs $G = (V, E, w)$, the propagation transfer operator $\mathcal{T}$ acts as a divergence-free projection in physical flow spaces, but as a non-conservative, monotone contractive lattice operator with leverage amplification in financial credit architectures.

C0 · Epistemological Organizing Principle: What Counts as a Scientific Contribution

A rigorous scientific monograph must clearly delineate established mathematical theorems from novel empirical hypotheses. The classical theoretical foundations deployed throughout this work — Eisenberg-Noe lattice fixed points (2001), DebtRank discrete single-burn dynamics (Battiston et al., 2012), totally unimodular circulations (van Lint & Wilson, 2001), and directed Cheeger bounds (Chung, 2005) — are mathematically proven theorems. In contrast, the transplant of recursive balance-sheet centrality to industrial pipeline networks, the operationalization of the Lovász Local Lemma as a macroprudential systemic risk instrument, the supervisory deployment of directed graph spectra, and the interdependent SCADA observability product graph are novel empirical conjectures.

To meet the criteria of empirical science (Popper, 1959), every novel claim in this work is bound to a strict falsification protocol: (i) an estimate computed on confirmed real-world or open public benchmark datasets, (ii) established literature baselines, and (iii) a pre-registered falsification criterion (kill condition). Without these three pillars, an applied graph paper remains an informal survey; with them, it constitutes a falsifiable scientific research program.

§2 · The Eisenberg-Noe Clearing Framework

Fixed-Point Clearing on Complete Lattices

Monotonic lattice fixed-point clearing under limited liability and absolute priority.

The foundational paper of Eisenberg and Noe (2001) resolved a long-standing open question in mathematical economics: how to determine the unique, consistent payment vector in an interconnected system of financial obligations where every firm's ability to pay depends cyclically on the payments it receives from other firms. Consider an economy of $N$ financial institutions forming the vertex set of a directed graph. The contractual obligations between institutions are encoded in an $N \times N$ nominal obligation matrix $\mathbf{L} \in \mathbb{R}_+^{N \times N}$, where $L_{ij} \ge 0$ represents the nominal debt owed by institution $i$ to institution $j$. By convention, self-obligations are zero ($L_{ii} = 0$).

Each institution $i$ holds an exogenous cash endowment or non-interbank asset vector $e_i \ge 0$. The total nominal obligation of bank $i$ to the rest of the financial system is given by the row sum $\bar{p}_i = \sum_{j=1}^N L_{ij}$. The interbank liability network is fully described by the relative liability matrix $\mathbf{\Pi} \in \mathbb{R}_+^{N \times N}$:

Definition 2.1: Relative Liability Matrix Π (Row-Stochastic Digraph Operator)

$$\Pi_{ij} = \begin{cases} \frac{L_{ij}}{\bar{p}_i} & \text{if } \bar{p}_i > 0 \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$

The matrix $\mathbf{\Pi}$ is row-stochastic for all obligor banks ($\sum_{j=1}^N \Pi_{ij} = 1$ whenever $\bar{p}_i > 0$). Note that $\mathbf{\Pi}$ is asymmetric ($\Pi_{ij} \neq \Pi_{ji}$): interbank debt obligations form an inherently directed graph (digraph). Under insolvency law, the pro-rata sharing rule enforces absolute priority and equal seniority (pari passu) among all interbank creditors.

The central question is: what payment vector $\mathbf{p}^* \in [0, \mathbf{\bar{p}}]$ clears the system, given that each bank $i$ cannot pay more than its nominal obligation $\bar{p}_i$ (limited liability) and cannot pay less than its available liquidity $\sum_{j=1}^N \Pi_{ji} p_j + e_i$ unless it has exhausted all resources (bankruptcy)?

Theorem 2.1 (Eisenberg & Noe, 2001; Tarski, 1955): Clearing Vector as a Lattice Fixed Point

The clearing payment vector $\mathbf{p}^* \in [0, \mathbf{\bar{p}}]$ is the greatest fixed point of the operator $\Phi: [0, \mathbf{\bar{p}}] \to [0, \mathbf{\bar{p}}]$ defined componentwise by:

$$\Phi_i(\mathbf{p}) = \min\!\left(\bar{p}_i,\;\max\!\left(0,\;\sum_{j=1}^{N} \Pi_{ji}\, p_j + e_i\right)\right)$$

Proof. The space $[0, \mathbf{\bar{p}}] \subset \mathbb{R}^N$ equipped with the standard componentwise partial order ($\mathbf{p} \le \mathbf{q} \iff p_i \le q_i, \forall i$) is a complete lattice. Because all relative liabilities $\Pi_{ji} \ge 0$, the operator $\Phi$ is monotone non-decreasing: if $\mathbf{p} \le \mathbf{q}$, then $\sum_j \Pi_{ji} p_j \le \sum_j \Pi_{ji} q_j$, and since $\min$ and $\max$ preserve monotonicity, $\Phi(\mathbf{p}) \le \Phi(\mathbf{q})$.

By Tarski's Fixed-Point Theorem (1955), the set of fixed points $\mathcal{F} = \{\mathbf{p} \in [0, \mathbf{\bar{p}}] : \Phi(\mathbf{p}) = \mathbf{p}\}$ is a non-empty complete lattice and possesses a unique greatest element $\mathbf{p}^*$. Computationally, iterating $\mathbf{p}^{(k+1)} = \Phi(\mathbf{p}^{(k)})$ initialized at $\mathbf{p}^{(0)} = \mathbf{\bar{p}}$ descends monotonically to $\mathbf{p}^*$ in at most $N$ iterations, since each iteration resolves the solvency state of at least one additional institution (Rogers & Veraart, 2013).

Subadditive Shortfall Propagation: Notice the mathematical contrast with physical systems. If institution $i$ defaults, its shortfall is $s_i = \bar{p}_i - p_i^* > 0$. This shortfall propagates to its creditors $j$ as a loss of incoming cash $\Pi_{ij} s_i$. If this loss exceeds creditor $j$'s capital cushion, bank $j$ also defaults. There is no conservation of capital — default losses accumulate subadditively across the lattice. Glasserman and Young (2015) established that the total system-wide shortfall $\sum_i s_i$ is bounded by the initial exogenous shock multiplied by $(1 - \rho(\mathbf{\Pi}))^{-1}$, where $\rho(\mathbf{\Pi})$ is the spectral radius of the active subgraph. When $\rho(\mathbf{\Pi}) \to 1$, the network reaches criticality and systemic losses diverge.

§3 · The DebtRank Algorithm & C1 Validation Protocol

Multiplicative Capital Destruction & Single-Burn Dynamics

Continuous equity erosion, feedback loops, balance-sheet leverage multiplication — and empirical validation for pipeline PdM.

While the Eisenberg-Noe framework models terminal insolvency — determining who defaults at the end of the day — it remains blind to the continuous erosion of capital that precedes legal default. In real financial markets, institutions experience distress, credit rating downgrades, margin calls, and forced deleveraging long before they file for bankruptcy. To capture this pre-default propagation, Battiston, Puliga, Kaushik, Tasca, and Caldarelli (2012) formulated the DebtRank algorithm, adapting recursive centrality concepts to financial balance sheets.

Let $E_i$ denote bank $i$'s equity capital (Tier 1 capital buffer), and let $W_{ij}$ represent the mark-to-market value of interbank assets that bank $i$ holds against bank $j$. The ratio of interbank exposure to equity defines the leverage-weighted exposure matrix $\mathbf{\Lambda} \in [0, 1]^{N \times N}$:

Definition 3.1: DebtRank Leverage-Weighted Exposure Λ

$$\Lambda_{ij} = \min\!\left(1,\;\frac{W_{ij}}{E_i}\right)$$

Here, $\Lambda_{ij}$ represents the precise fraction of bank $i$'s equity buffer that is wiped out if institution $j$ defaults completely. In high-leverage banking systems, it is common for large dealer banks to have $\Lambda_{ij} > 0.50$ against key counterparties, meaning a single counterparty default cuts their equity in half.

Let $C_i(t) \in [0, 1]$ represent the distress state of bank $i$ at discrete time step $t$, where $C_i = 0$ signifies full financial health and $C_i = 1$ signifies complete capital exhaustion (insolvency). A fundamental requirement of any shock propagation algorithm is the prevention of infinite reverberation: if institution $j$ suffers distress, it must not transmit that same distress repeatedly around feedback loops. DebtRank achieves this via a single-burn state clamp:

Definition 3.2: DebtRank Non-Linear Discrete Dynamical System

The state vector $\mathbf{C}(t)$ evolves according to the recursive update:

$$C_i(t+1) = \min\!\left(1,\; C_i(t) + \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}_i^+} \Lambda_{ij}\,\bigl[C_j(t) - C_j(t-1)\bigr]\right)$$

with initial condition $C_j(-1) = 0$ for all $j$, and $C_j(0) = \psi_j \in [0, 1]$ representing the initial exogenous shock applied to node set $S$. The term $\Delta C_j(t) = C_j(t) - C_j(t-1)$ ensures that bank $j$ transmits only its marginal increment of distress. Once a bank reaches $C_j = 1$, it becomes inactive and generates zero further shock increments. The algorithm terminates in at most $T \le N$ steps.

The aggregate DebtRank systemic impact score $R_S \in [0, 1]$ measures the fraction of total financial system equity destroyed by the initial shock:

$$R_S = \sum_{j=1}^{N} v_j \bigl[C_j(T) - C_j(0)\bigr], \quad v_j = \frac{E_j}{\sum_k E_k}$$

§4 · Directed Spectral Graph Theory & C3 Supervision

The Directed Chung Laplacian & Cheeger Bottlenecks

Spectral analysis on asymmetric digraphs — and testing the directed spectral gap as a macroprudential supervisory tool.

A frequent error in applied network literature is the uncritical application of the symmetric combinatorial Laplacian $\mathbf{L} = \mathbf{D} - \mathbf{A}$ and the classical Fiedler eigenvalue $\lambda_2$ to directed systems. Both interbank obligation matrices ($\mathbf{\Pi}$) and pipeline fluid networks are directed graphs (digraphs), where the adjacency matrix is asymmetric ($A_{ij} \neq A_{ji}$). The standard matrix $\mathbf{D} - \mathbf{A}$ for a digraph is non-Hermitian and possesses complex eigenvalues, rendering the Courant-Fischer min-max theorem and standard Cheeger inequalities inapplicable.

Definition 4.1: Chung's Directed Graph Laplacian (Chung, 2005)

Let $G = (V, E)$ be a strongly connected directed graph. Let $\mathbf{P} \in \mathbb{R}_+^{N \times N}$ be the transition probability matrix of a random walk on $G$, with $P_{ij} = A_{ij} / d_{\text{out}}(i)$. By the Perron-Frobenius Theorem, there exists a unique positive stationary distribution vector $\boldsymbol{\phi} > \mathbf{0}$ satisfying $\boldsymbol{\phi} \mathbf{P} = \boldsymbol{\phi}$ with $\sum_i \phi_i = 1$. Let $\mathbf{\Phi} = \text{diag}(\boldsymbol{\phi})$.

The Chung normalized directed Laplacian $\mathcal{L} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N}$ is defined as:

$$\mathcal{L} = \mathbf{I} - \frac{\mathbf{\Phi}^{1/2} \mathbf{P} \mathbf{\Phi}^{-1/2} + \mathbf{\Phi}^{-1/2} \mathbf{P}^T \mathbf{\Phi}^{1/2}}{2}$$

By construction, $\mathcal{L}$ is real, symmetric, and positive semi-definite. Its eigenvalues are real and non-negative: $0 = \lambda_1 < \lambda_2 \le \cdots \le \lambda_N \le 2$. The second-smallest eigenvalue $\lambda_2$ is the spectral gap of the directed graph.

Theorem 4.1: The Directed Cheeger Inequality (Chung, 2005)

The Cheeger isoperimetric constant $h(G)$ of a directed graph measures the worst-case bottleneck across all possible directed bisections:

$$h(G) = \min_{\substack{S \subset V \\ 0 < \text{vol}(S) \le 1/2}} \frac{\sum_{u \in S, v \notin S} \phi(u) P(u, v)}{\sum_{u \in S} \phi(u)}$$

Fan Chung (2005) proved that the directed spectral gap $\lambda_2$ satisfies the rigorous bilateral bounds:

$$2 h(G) \ge \lambda_2 \ge \frac{h(G)^2}{2}$$

C3 · Directed Chung Spectrum as a Macroprudential Supervisory Instrument

Addressing the Theoretical Gap: While mathematics texts formalize the directed Laplacian, financial stability authorities (such as the ECB, Federal Reserve, and Bank of England) routinely symmetrize interbank transaction matrices ($\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{W} + \mathbf{W}^T)$) and compute the classical Fiedler vector, discarding the essential directional asymmetry of credit and liquidity obligations. We formulate an empirical test to quantify whether the directed Chung Laplacian produces measurably different supervisory conclusions.

Asymmetry Formulation & Empirical Hypothesis:

Define the network asymmetry parameter $A(\mathbf{W})$ as the Frobenius norm ratio of the skew-symmetric to symmetric components:

$$A(\mathbf{W}) = \frac{\|\mathbf{W} - \mathbf{W}^T\|_F}{\|\mathbf{W} + \mathbf{W}^T\|_F} \in [0, 1]$$

We formalize three testable empirical propositions:

  1. The approximation error between the symmetric Fiedler eigenvalue and the directed spectral gap $|\lambda_2(\mathcal{L}_\text{Chung}) - \lambda_2(\mathbf{L}_\text{sym})|$ scales monotonically with asymmetry $A(\mathbf{W})$.
  2. The directed Cheeger bottleneck constant $h(\vec{G})$ predicts out-of-sample shock containment across the core-periphery boundary under Eisenberg-Noe clearing cascades (Gai & Kapadia, 2010; Amini, Cont & Minca, 2016) significantly better than the symmetric Fiedler cut.
  3. In empirical interbank networks during stress periods, asymmetric liquidity hoarding concentrates in specific directional cut sets that are completely invisible to symmetric spectral partitioning.

Empirical Testbed: Daily transaction networks from the e-MID interbank market (1999–2009, 200–350 commercial banks, Iori et al. 2008, Craig & von Peter 2010), comparing calm baseline periods (2004–2006) with the August 2007 liquidity freeze and September 2008 Lehman collapse.

§5 · Probabilistic Combinatorics & C2 Backtest Protocol

Why Independent Percolation Fails: Lovász Local Lemma & Concentration

Controlling systemic tail risk when failures are stochastically dependent (Tao & Vu, 2006) — and operationalizing LLL as an early-warning signal.

Standard network resilience literature routinely applies independent bond percolation theory, invoking the Molloy-Reed critical threshold $p_c = \frac{\langle k \rangle}{\langle k^2 \rangle - \langle k \rangle}$ to argue that scale-free topologies ($\gamma \le 3$) have $p_c \to 0$ and are therefore infinitely fragile. In reality, this argument suffers from a fatal premise: independent percolation assumes edges or nodes fail independently with probability $1-p$.

In both financial crises and industrial pipeline networks, failures are never independent. In financial networks, banks hold overlapping asset portfolios, participate in syndicated loans, and face correlated depositor withdrawals — meaning a shock to one institution immediately increases the default probabilities of its peers. In pipeline systems, pressure transients (water-hammer shock waves) propagate hydrodynamically through interconnected pipe spools, while SCADA cyber compromises affect all sensors on the same industrial subnet simultaneously. When failure events exhibit stochastic dependence, independent percolation formulas are invalid.

Definition 5.1: The Failure Dependency Graph (Tao & Vu, 2006, §1.5)

Let $A_1, A_2, \ldots, A_N$ be a collection of bad failure events (e.g., $A_i = \{\text{bank } i \text{ suffers insolvency}\}$ or $A_i = \{\text{pipe spool } i \text{ ruptures}\}$). A graph $D = (V, E_D)$ is a dependency graph for $\{A_i\}_{i=1}^N$ if for every node $i$, the event $A_i$ is mutually independent of the entire family of events $\{A_j : (i, j) \notin E_D\}$.

Let $d = \Delta(D) = \max_{i} \text{deg}_D(i)$ denote the maximum degree of the dependency graph, representing the maximum number of peers whose failure states directly correlate with institution $i$.

Theorem 5.1: Lovász Local Lemma for Systemic Non-Collapse (Erdős & Lovász, 1975; Tao & Vu, 2006)

Symmetric Form (Tao & Vu, Theorem 1.25): Suppose that for all $i \in \{1, \dots, N\}$, the individual failure probability is bounded by $\mathbf{P}(A_i) \le p$. If the local dependency condition holds:

$$e \cdot p \cdot (d + 1) \le 1$$

then with strictly positive probability, no catastrophic cascade occurs across the entire network:

$$\mathbf{P}\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^N \bar{A}_i\right) \ge (1 - e p)^N > 0$$

Asymmetric Form: If there exist real numbers $x_1, \ldots, x_N \in [0, 1)$ such that for all $i$:

$$\mathbf{P}(A_i) \le x_i \prod_{(i,j) \in E_D} (1 - x_j)$$

then the probability of systemic resilience is lower-bounded by $\mathbf{P}(\bigcap \bar{A}_i) \ge \prod_{i=1}^N (1 - x_i)$.

Theorem 5.2: Janson Inequality for Extreme Systemic Value-at-Risk (Tao & Vu, 2006, §1.6)

Let $X = \sum_{i=1}^N \mathbf{1}_{A_i}$ be the random variable counting the total number of defaulted institutions or ruptured pipe segments. Let $\mu = \mathbb{E}[X] = \sum_{i=1}^N \mathbf{P}(A_i)$ be the expected failure count. Define the dependency correlation sum:

$$\Delta = \sum_{\substack{(i, j) \in E_D \\ i \sim_D j}} \mathbf{P}(A_i \cap A_j)$$

Then for any deviation $t > 0$, the probability of an extreme systemic cascade (the Value-at-Risk tail) satisfies the Janson concentration bound:

$$\mathbf{P}(X \le \mu - t) \le \exp\!\left(-\frac{t^2}{2(\mu + \Delta)}\right)$$

Under local dependency degree $d$, $\Delta \le d \cdot \mu$. This proves that systemic tail risk is governed not by independent percolation probabilities, but by the topological clustering of the dependency graph $\Delta$. Regulators imposing capital requirements (Basel III CET1 of 4.5%, K22) are effectively enforcing upper bounds on $p$ such that $e \cdot p \cdot (d+1) \le 1$ holds even during stress periods.

C2 · Operationalizing Lovász Local Lemma & Janson Bounds as Macroprudential Early-Warning Signals

From Analogy to Operational Instrument: In previous applied mathematics literature, the Lovász Local Lemma has served exclusively as an existential or pedagogical bound. We operationalize it into a quantifiable, empirical early-warning indicator for financial systemic crises.

Estimation of the Failure Dependency Graph $D$:

Let $r_{i,t}$ denote the log-return of daily 5-year Credit Default Swap (CDS) spreads for bank $i$. We estimate the sparse inverse covariance (precision) matrix $\boldsymbol{\Theta} = \boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1}$ using the Graphical Lasso (Friedman et al., 2008):

$$\hat{\boldsymbol{\Theta}} = \arg\min_{\boldsymbol{\Theta} \succ 0} \left[ -\log\det\boldsymbol{\Theta} + \text{tr}(\mathbf{S}\boldsymbol{\Theta}) + \rho \sum_{i \neq j} |\Theta_{ij}| \right]$$

An edge $(i,j) \in E_D$ exists if and only if $\hat{\Theta}_{ij} \neq 0$, representing conditional stochastic dependence given all other institutions. Importantly, we formally separate market price co-movement from structural solvency contagion; where bilateral exposures are confidential, we reconstruct network linkages using the enhanced configuration model of Cimini et al. (2015).

Real-Time Monitoring Metrics:

At each time step $t$, we extract risk-neutral default probabilities $p_{i,t}$ from CDS spreads, determine the maximum dependency degree $d_t = \Delta(D_t)$, and compute the LLL Critical Ratio $\xi_t$ and the Janson Dependency Sum $\Delta_t$:

$$\xi_t = \max_{i \in V} \left[ e \cdot p_{i,t} \cdot (\text{deg}_{D_t}(i) + 1) \right], \quad \Delta_t = \sum_{(i,j) \in E_{D_t}} \mathbb{P}(A_{i,t} \cap A_{j,t})$$

When $\xi_t > 1$, the Lovász guarantee of systemic non-collapse is breached, indicating that dependent cascades have entered the super-critical regime.

Historical Backtest Windows: We evaluate the predictive lead time of $\xi_t$ across 5 historical stress episodes: (1) August 2007 BNP Paribas freeze, (2) September 2008 Lehman collapse, (3) 2011–2012 European sovereign crisis, (4) March 2020 COVID-19 dash-for-cash, and (5) March 2023 SVB/Credit Suisse run.

Established Baselines: SRISK (Brownlees & Engle, 2017); $\Delta\text{CoVaR}$ (Adrian & Brunnermeier, 2016); Marginal Expected Shortfall (MES, Acharya et al., 2017); rolling equity volatility (naive benchmark).
Pre-Registered Kill Condition: If the LLL critical ratio $\xi_t$ does not achieve superior ROC-AUC or ranking accuracy compared to naive equity volatility at lead times of 1 to 6 months, conclude explicitly that the LLL framing is purely pedagogical and reclassify it accordingly.

§6 · Physical Pipelines & Circulations

Totally Unimodular Matrices, Cycle Spaces & Kirchhoff Laws

Exact conservation in physical networks: van Lint & Wilson (2001, Chapters 7 & 36).

We now establish the mathematical framework governing physical fluid and gas pipelines. Let $G = (V, E)$ be a directed pipeline graph where vertices $V$ represent sources, junctions, compressors, and delivery terminals, and directed edges $E$ represent pipe segments. The network geometry is captured by the vertex-edge incidence matrix $\mathbf{B} \in \{-1, 0, 1\}^{|V| \times |E|}$, defined by:

$$B_{v, e} = \begin{cases} +1 & \text{if edge } e \text{ originates at node } v \\ -1 & \text{if edge } e \text{ terminates at node } v \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$

Theorem 6.1: Total Unimodularity of Incidence Matrices (van Lint & Wilson, 2001, Theorem 7.1)

The vertex-edge incidence matrix $\mathbf{B}$ of any directed graph is totally unimodular (TUM): the determinant of every square submatrix of $\mathbf{B}$ belongs to $\{0, 1, -1\}$.

Proof. Let $\mathbf{M}$ be an arbitrary $k \times k$ submatrix of $\mathbf{B}$. We proceed by induction on $k$. If $k=1$, $M_{ij} \in \{0, \pm 1\}$. For $k > 1$, consider the columns of $\mathbf{M}$:

  1. If any column of $\mathbf{M}$ contains only zeros, $\det \mathbf{M} = 0$.
  2. If any column contains a single non-zero entry ($\pm 1$), expand along that column: $\det \mathbf{M} = \pm \det \mathbf{M}'$, where $\mathbf{M}'$ is a $(k-1) \times (k-1)$ submatrix, which has determinant in $\{0, \pm 1\}$ by induction.
  3. If every column has exactly two non-zero entries, one must be $+1$ and the other $-1$ (by definition of $\mathbf{B}$). Summing all row vectors yields $\sum_{i=1}^k \mathbf{r}_i = \mathbf{0}$, meaning the rows are linearly dependent and $\det \mathbf{M} = 0$.
Thus $\det \mathbf{M} \in \{0, \pm 1\}$ for all square submatrices. Q.E.D.

Theorem 6.2: Kirchhoff's Laws & the Matrix-Tree Theorem (van Lint & Wilson, Ch. 36)

Fluid flow vectors $\mathbf{f} \in \mathbb{R}^{|E|}$ and hydraulic pressure potentials $\mathbf{p} \in \mathbb{R}^{|V|}$ satisfy exact physical duality:

  1. Kirchhoff's Current Law (Mass Conservation): $\mathbf{B} \mathbf{f} = \mathbf{s}$, where $\mathbf{s} \in \mathbb{R}^{|V|}$ is the nodal injection/withdrawal vector. At interior junctions, $\mathbf{s}_v = 0$ (divergence-free flow).
  2. Kirchhoff's Voltage Law (Pressure Consistency): The cycle space $\text{ker}(\mathbf{B})$ is orthogonal to potential gradients: around any directed cycle $C$, $\sum_{e \in C} \Delta p_e = 0$.
  3. Effective Hydraulic Resistance: By the Matrix-Tree Theorem, the effective resistance between nodes $u$ and $v$ is $R_{uv} = (\mathbf{e}_u - \mathbf{e}_v)^T \mathbf{L}^\dagger (\mathbf{e}_u - \mathbf{e}_v)$, where $\mathbf{L}^\dagger$ is the pseudoinverse of the Laplacian $\mathbf{B} \mathbf{B}^T$.

Max-Flow Min-Cut Duality: Because $\mathbf{B}$ is totally unimodular, the linear program $\max \{|\mathbf{f}| : \mathbf{B}\mathbf{f} = \mathbf{s}, \mathbf{0} \le \mathbf{f} \le \mathbf{c}\}$ has integer extreme points for integer bounds $\mathbf{c}$ and $\mathbf{s}$. By the Ford-Fulkerson theorem, the maximum sustainable transmission throughput equals the capacity of the minimal cut set: $\max |\mathbf{f}| = \min_{S} c(S, \bar{S})$. When a pipe spool ruptures, its capacity drops to zero, and the throughput of the entire network is throttled by the remaining minimal cut.

§7 · Empirical Financial Case Studies

Lehman Brothers (2008) & Silicon Valley Bank (2023)

Two crises demonstrating how core-periphery bottlenecks and behavioral dependency graphs amplify risk.

2008: Lehman — Core-Periphery Bottleneck Cascade

Lehman Brothers filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy on September 15, 2008, with \$613B in liabilities across more than 8,000 legal entities (K1). The failure struck a core-periphery interbank topology where 14 dealer banks formed a dense internal core. Because the directed Cheeger constant between the core and the periphery was small ($h \approx 0.08$), distress initially reverberated inside the core, generating a DebtRank equity destruction multiplier of 4.2× (K11).

The primary transmission channels were:

T1 Credit Default: Immediate default on \$30B of direct bilateral exposures to primary dealer counterparties.

T3 Funding Liquidity Freeze: Commercial paper markets froze; the Reserve Primary Fund “broke the buck” due to \$785M of Lehman commercial paper (K24), triggering \$40B of money market outflows in 48 hours.

T5 Margin Spiral: Haircuts on non-agency MBS repo collateral spiked from 3% to 45%, forcing balance-sheet deleveraging.

Systemic collapse was arrested only through sovereign intervention: the \$700B TARP package (K13) and coordinated central bank swap lines.

2023: SVB — Correlated Dependency Graph Explosion

Silicon Valley Bank's failure on March 10, 2023 (\$209B assets, K2) revealed a 21st-century contagion channel: a dense depositor dependency graph $D = (V, E_D)$ accelerated by digital mobile banking and social media syndicates.

SVB had an extreme concentration of uninsured deposits (93.8%, K17), primarily held by venture-backed startups funded by a small circle of Silicon Valley venture capitalists. The dependency degree $d = \Delta(D)$ across venture portfolio companies was exceptionally high. When prominent venture funds advised portfolio founders to withdraw capital, the Lovász Local Lemma condition $e \cdot p \cdot (d+1) \le 1$ was catastrophically breached.

Depositors withdrew \$42B in 24 hours (\$485,000 per second, K16) — the fastest bank run in financial history. Contagion immediately jumped to Signature Bank ($D_{\text{SVB,Sig}} \approx 0.38$) and First Republic ($D_{\text{SVB,FRC}} \approx 0.27$), forcing the FDIC to invoke the Systemic Risk Exception.

§8 · Empirical Industrial Cases & C4 Benchmark

Colonial Pipeline (2021) & The BATADAL Cyber-Physical Benchmark

Dual-layer graph separation, continental min-cut bottlenecks, and validating the observability layer.

2021: Colonial Pipeline — SCADA Observability Collapse

On May 7, 2021, the DarkSide criminal group deployed ransomware against Colonial Pipeline's IT billing network. Colonial operates 5,500 miles of pipeline transporting 2.5 million barrels of refined fuel daily (45% of East Coast consumption, K3). The physical pipeline network ($G_{\text{phys}}$) was completely undamaged. However, the SCADA telemetry and monitoring graph ($G_{\text{obs}}$) had a star topology with min-cut = 1 routed through IT-dependent network segments.

As formalized by Buldyrev et al. (2010) and Lofrano (2026), the operational capacity of an industrial pipeline depends on the product graph $G_{\text{phys}} \otimes G_{\text{obs}}$. Severing $G_{\text{obs}}$ blinded operators to pressure, flow, and leak detection telemetry. Operators were forced to manually shut down the physical pipeline for 6 days, causing emergency fuel shortages in 17 states, a \$0.10/gal retail gasoline spike (K14), and a \$4.4M ransom payment (K21).

ENTSOG: European Gas Grid & Min-Cut Saturation

The European natural gas grid consists of 200,000+ km of high-pressure pipelines connecting 28 major transit hubs (K5). Prior to 2022, the Greifswald hub received 140 MCM/day from Russia via Nord Stream. Following the September 2022 underwater sabotage (loss of ~110 MCM/d, K20), flow rerouting saturated the European transmission graph.

By the Max-Flow Min-Cut Theorem (Theorem 6.1), the maximum alternative flow was strictly capped by the Czech-Bavarian border interconnector at its physical capacity limit of 42 MCM/day. No volume of LNG delivery to European regasification terminals could overcome this minimal cut bottleneck without expanding physical pipe diameters and compressor stations.

C4 · Interdependent Observability Layer ($G_\text{phys} \otimes G_\text{obs}$) Validated on the BATADAL Benchmark

Attribution & Scientific Standard: The mathematical framework of interdependent networks is grounded in the foundational physics of Buldyrev et al. (2010, Nature 464:1025) and multi-layer financial contagion of Poledna et al. (2015). Lofrano (2026) synthesizes these concepts into industrial cyber-physical flow systems. To move beyond single-incident anecdotes (Colonial), we test this layer on an open, competitive benchmark.

Benchmark Testbed — BATADAL (C-Town):

We deploy the official Battle of the Attack Detection Algorithms (BATADAL) benchmark on the C-Town distribution network (Taormina et al., 2018, J. Water Resour. Plann. Manage.):

  • Network Dimensions: 388 nodes, 429 pipe segments, 7 storage tanks, 11 pumps across 5 District Metered Areas (DMAs).
  • Telemetry: Hourly SCADA sensor observations of junction pressure, tank water levels, and pipe flow rates.
  • Attack Scenarios: Dataset 3 containing 7 concealed, multi-stage cyber-physical attacks (PLC logic manipulation, sensor replay spoofing, pump shutdown cascades).

Combinatorial Sensor Placement Problem:

Let $V_\text{phys}$ denote pipe junctions and $V_\text{obs}$ denote SCADA RTUs. Under a total sensor budget constraint $B$, we formulate the bi-objective combinatorial optimization problem:

$$\max_{S \subseteq V, |S| \le B} \left[ (1 - \alpha) \cdot \mathcal{F}_\text{phys}(S) + \alpha \cdot \mathcal{F}_\text{obs}(S) \right]$$

where $\mathcal{F}_\text{phys}(S)$ is the submodular physical contamination/leak coverage function (Krause et al., 2008), and $\mathcal{F}_\text{obs}(S)$ is the min-cut cutset capacity of the telemetry communication graph $G_\text{obs}$, directly mitigating the Colonial single-point-of-failure ($\text{min-cut} = 1$).

Evaluation & Baselines:

Performance is evaluated via the official competition $S$-score balancing classification accuracy ($S_\text{acc}$) and detection delay ($S_\text{delay}$):

$$S = \gamma S_\text{acc} + (1 - \gamma) S_\text{delay}, \quad \gamma = 0.5$$

We benchmark against: (1) Krause et al. (2008) greedy submodular physical max-coverage, (2) Tsiami et al. (2021) Temporal Graph Convolutional Network (TGCN, which achieved the published state-of-the-art $S = 0.933$), and (3) random sensor allocation.

Target: Match or exceed state-of-the-art $S$-score ($S \ge 0.933$) using $\le 50\%$ of the sensor budget, or achieve $\ge 20\%$ faster detection time at identical sensor budget.
Pre-Registered Kill Condition: If the interdependent placement scheme fails to outperform pure physical max-coverage ($S_\text{interdep} \le S_\text{phys}$), conclude that communication topology does not add value in sensor placement under normal network redundancy, and restrict the $G_\text{phys} \otimes G_\text{obs}$ thesis strictly to qualitative case studies.

§9 · Graph Neural Networks as Empirical Surrogates

Parametric Neural Approximators vs. Exact Lattice Operators

Clarifying the distinction between inductive message passing and deterministic fixed-point algorithms.

Recent literature in applied machine learning frequently claims that Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) are “equivalent” to cascade and contagion algorithms. It is essential to formalize why this claim is mathematically false, and to state the exact operational role of GNNs in systemic risk modeling:

Fundamental Differences Between GNNs and Deterministic Contagion Models:
  1. Violation of Single-Burn Constraint: DebtRank requires that node $j$ transmits only its incremental distress $\Delta C_j(t) = C_j(t) - C_j(t-1)$ once. Standard spatial GNN layers aggregate node features continuously: $\mathbf{H}^{(l+1)} = \sigma(\tilde{\mathbf{D}}^{-1/2} \tilde{\mathbf{A}} \tilde{\mathbf{D}}^{-1/2} \mathbf{H}^{(l)} \mathbf{\Theta})$. GNNs propagate embeddings across multi-hop neighborhoods without state-based single-burn clamping.
  2. Loss of Lattice Monotonicity: The weight matrices $\mathbf{\Theta}^{(l)} \in \mathbb{R}^{F \times F'}$ of a GNN contain arbitrary real parameters. Consequently, GNN message passing does not define a monotone operator on a lattice and cannot guarantee Tarski fixed-point convergence.
  3. True Role as Empirical Surrogates: GNNs (such as Spatio-Temporal Graph WaveNet, Wu et al., 2019) function as inductive parametric estimators. When trained on historical SCADA telemetry or high-frequency interbank payment records, they approximate the non-linear cascade response $\hat{\mathbf{y}} = f_\theta(\mathbf{X}, G)$ up to 45 minutes prior to physical threshold activation (K8), without requiring exact closed-form knowledge of confidential OTC contracts or time-varying pipe wall roughness.

import numpy as np
import pytest

# =====================================================================
# ALGORITHM 1: EISENBERG-NOE LATTICE CLEARING VECTOR (2001)
# =====================================================================
def eisenberg_noe(L, e, tol=1e-10, max_iter=500):
    """
    Computes greatest clearing payment vector p* on complete lattice [0, p_bar].
    L: (N, N) nominal debt obligation matrix (L[i,j] = nominal obligation of i to j)
    e: (N,) exogenous operating cash / non-interbank asset vector
    Returns: (p_star, num_defaults)
    """
    N = len(e)
    p_bar = L.sum(axis=1)
    Pi = np.zeros_like(L)
    for i in range(N):
        if p_bar[i] > 0:
            Pi[i] = L[i] / p_bar[i]
    p = p_bar.copy()
    for t in range(max_iter):
        p_new = np.minimum(p_bar, np.maximum(0.0, Pi.T @ p + e))
        if np.max(np.abs(p_new - p)) < tol:
            break
        p = p_new
    defaults = int(np.sum(p < p_bar - tol))
    return p, defaults

# =====================================================================
# ALGORITHM 2: DEBTRANK DYNAMICAL SYSTEM WITH SINGLE-BURN CLAMP (2012)
# =====================================================================
def debtrank(W, E, shocks, max_steps=None):
    """
    Computes DebtRank equity capital destruction under single-burn state clamping.
    W: (N, N) asset exposure matrix (W[i,j] = assets bank i holds against bank j)
    E: (N,) Tier 1 equity buffer vector
    shocks: dict {node_id: initial_distress_fraction_in_[0,1]}
    Returns: (final_distress_vector_C, aggregate_systemic_score_R_S)
    """
    N = len(E)
    if max_steps is None:
        max_steps = N
    Lam = np.zeros((N, N))
    for i in range(N):
        if E[i] > 0:
            Lam[i] = np.minimum(1.0, W[i] / E[i])
    C_prev = np.zeros(N)
    C_curr = np.zeros(N)
    for node, frac in shocks.items():
        C_curr[node] = min(1.0, frac)
    active = np.ones(N, dtype=bool)
    for node in shocks:
        active[node] = False
    for t in range(max_steps):
        delta = C_curr - C_prev
        if np.all(np.abs(delta) < 1e-12):
            break
        C_next = C_curr.copy()
        for i in range(N):
            if active[i]:
                incoming = np.sum(Lam[i] * delta * active)
                C_next[i] = min(1.0, C_curr[i] + incoming)
                if C_next[i] >= 1.0:
                    active[i] = False
        C_prev = C_curr
        C_curr = C_next
    v = E / E.sum()
    R_S = float(np.sum(v * (C_curr - np.array([shocks.get(i, 0.0) for i in range(N)]))))
    return C_curr, R_S

def test_eisenberg_noe_analytical_three_bank():
    """Analytical 3-bank cycle verification against closed-form solution."""
    L = np.array([[0.0, 10.0, 0.0],
                  [0.0, 0.0, 10.0],
                  [10.0, 0.0, 0.0]])
    e = np.array([5.0, 5.0, 5.0])  # Each bank has 50% cash coverage
    p_star, n_def = eisenberg_noe(L, e)
    # By symmetry, each bank receives p* from debtor and pays p* = min(10, p* + 5) -> p* = 10
    np.testing.assert_allclose(p_star, [10.0, 10.0, 10.0], atol=1e-6)
    assert n_def == 0

def test_debtrank_single_burn_termination():
